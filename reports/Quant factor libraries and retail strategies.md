# Beyond Alpha Lists: Replicate Rigorously, Trade Structurally

The most valuable resources beyond *101 Formulaic Alphas*, Alpha191 and *151 Trading Strategies* are not longer formula lists but maintained replication libraries and the meta-research that tells you what any formula is worth: globally, Chen and Zimmermann's Open Source Asset Pricing (200+ US predictors, with a new release in October 2026), the Jensen–Kelly–Pedersen global factor data (153 factors in 93 countries) and Hou–Xue–Zhang's 452-anomaly replication; for A-shares, the Liu–Stambaugh–Yuan CH-3/CH-4 models, a 469-anomaly China replication and the broker 金融工程 (financial-engineering) report series of 海通, 华泰, 开源 and others, where most A-share factor knowledge actually lives. Their verdict is sobering: net of costs and data mining, the average published US anomaly earns about 4 basis points a month, and 83–87% of the 469 China anomalies are insignificant once microcaps stop dominating the weights. Alpha191 itself was built for 30–50x annual turnover, and its printed formulas contain errors that leave roughly 10–15 factors open to interpretation. Beyond stock selection, the approaches that suit individuals exploit what institutions cannot do — trade without capacity limits, ignore benchmarks, sit through flat years, use cheap exchange leverage — rather than speed: diversified trend following (the SG Trend Index has compounded 5.75% a year since 2000 and gained 27% in 2022), trend-filtered and volatility-targeted allocation used for drawdown control, conservatively sized index option writing, and carry inside a diversified futures book. China adds its own structural menu — convertible-bond 双低 ("double-low") rotation (13.7% a year in 2018–2024), IPO and convertible-bond subscription, reverse-repo rate spikes, commodity CTA and dividend low-volatility — but every item is now under pressure from a shrinking convertible-bond universe, real defaults, crowding crashes, the July 2024 suspension of securities lending and program-trading reporting rules that reach individuals. Strategies that live on speed, order-flow position or cheap shorting — HFT, 0DTE options, short-horizon stat arb, limit-up chasing — have uniformly negative evidence for retail traders.

## Open replication libraries now outrank formula lists

The three resources you already use share one format: a fixed catalogue of signals or strategies, tested in-sample and frozen at publication. Since the mid-2010s the field has built a more useful layer on top — maintained datasets that reproduce hundreds of published anomalies with open code and current data — and that layer answers what a list cannot: whether a signal still works, whether it is a microcap artifact, and which known theme it duplicates. **Chen and Zimmermann's Open Source Asset Pricing (OSAP)** began from 319 characteristics in earlier meta-studies and reproduced 98% of the 161 "clear predictors" with t-statistics above 1.96 ([CFR paper](https://cfr.ivo-welch.info/published/papers/chen2021open.pdf)); it now covers "over 200 cross-sectional predictors" in eight categories from accounting data to options and 13F holdings ([R manual](https://opensourceap.r-universe.dev/OpenSourceAP.DownloadR/OpenSourceAP.DownloadR.pdf); [FAQ](https://www.openassetpricing.com/faq/)), and its **v2.1.0 release of 27–28 October 2026** moved to CRSP's new CIZ files and publishes parquet through Hugging Face, a year after v2.0.0 rewrote every signal from Stata into Python ([GitHub releases](https://github.com/OpenSourceAP/CrossSection/releases); [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/OpenSourceAP/CrossSection)). **Jensen, Kelly and Pedersen's Global Factor Data** spans **153 factors in 93 countries grouped into 13 themes**, finds that most factors replicate and hold up out of sample, and runs through December 2025; its Common Task Framework now hosts a public leaderboard for asset-pricing models built on a shared dataset and a fixed metric, with per-model pages added in October 2026 ([NBER WP](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28432/w28432.pdf); [jkpfactors.com](https://jkpfactors.com/)). **Hou, Xue and Zhang's "Replicating Anomalies"** is the sceptic's counterweight: with NYSE breakpoints and value weights, **65% of 452 anomalies fail a single-test hurdle and 82% fail a 2.78 multiple-test hurdle** ([RFS](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/33/5/2019/5236964)), and its global-q site refreshed the q and q5 factors and testing portfolios through December 2025 on 30 July 2026 ([global-q](https://global-q.org/index.html)).

These libraries do not contradict each other; they answer different questions. OSAP asks whether a paper's own construction can be reproduced, while HXZ asks whether the effect survives once microcaps stop dominating equal-weighted sorts, so running a signal through both is the cheapest available test of whether it is tradable. Two housekeeping facts matter when mixing sources. French's library moved US returns to CRSP CIZ with its January 2025 release ([French Data Library](https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/pages/faculty/ken.french/data_library.html)) and OSAP followed in October 2026, so historical series shift between vintages and every study should pin its release. And several well-known factor sets are frozen — Stambaugh–Yuan at 2016, Daniel–Hirshleifer–Sun at 2023 — so recent work has to rebuild them from OSAP or JKP characteristics. Otherwise the free benchmark kit is complete: AQR's betting-against-beta factor covers the US plus 23 markets and its time-series momentum factors 58 futures and forwards from 1985 ([AQR TSMOM](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Datasets/Time-Series-Momentum-Factors-Monthly)); Barillas and Shanken's model comparison, which finds the value factor redundant and momentum crucial, shows how to assemble a six-factor benchmark from French, global-q and AQR parts ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w21771.pdf)); and Baltussen, Swinkels and van Vliet's 217-year multi-asset sample supplies the longest out-of-sample check, in which most premiums persist with limited decay ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3325720)).

The direct descendants of *101 Formulaic Alphas* live at WorldQuant. Its BRAIN platform is free to join and expresses alphas in a "Fast Expression" language of operators over data fields, though only research consultants can simulate non-US markets ([quantt review](https://www.quantt.co.uk/resources/worldquant-brain)); WorldQuant reported **about 375,000 users and 13,000 research consultants** in March 2026, and the consultant programme advertises 100,000+ data fields from 8+ regions ([BRAIN consultant program](https://platform.worldquantbrain.com/consultant-program)). *Finding Alphas* (2nd edition, 2019) is its methodology book, with added chapters on alpha correlation, bias control, intraday data and machine learning ([O'Reilly](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/finding-alphas-2nd/9781119571216/)). Two Kakushadze papers belong beside the 101 formulas: a study of about 4,000 real alphas with 0.7–19-day holding periods, in which returns show no significant dependence on turnover and costs per share scale as 1/turnover ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1509.08110)), and "How to Combine a Billion Alphas," on turning many faint, short-lived signals into one combined mega-alpha ([ar5iv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1603.05937)). In the encyclopedia role of *151 Trading Strategies*, Quantpedia is the maintained analogue, with about 70 free strategies and more than 900 in its Premium tier ([pricing](https://quantpedia.com/pricing-pro/)), while Numerai offers the opposite of a formula list — obfuscated features in weekly eras, predicting roughly 20-day returns ([Numerai docs](https://docs.numer.ai/)).

The machine-learning era added standard feature sets and model code rather than more formulas. Gu, Kelly and Xiu's **920 covariates** — 94 characteristics interacted with 8 macro predictors, plus 74 industry dummies — remain the default ML benchmark, with a public file running to 2021 ([datashare.zip](https://dachxiu.chicagobooth.edu/download/datashare.zip)). The leading models ship code: IPCA ([bkelly-lab/ipca](https://github.com/bkelly-lab/ipca)), Chen–Pelger–Zhu's GAN-based pricing kernel ([INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/suppl/10.1287/mnsc.2023.4695)) and Bryzgalova–Pelger–Zhu's AP-Trees, whose tree-built test portfolios reach **up to 3x the out-of-sample Sharpe ratios of standard sorts**, implying that decile spreads set too low a bar ([RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v80y2025i5p2447-2506.html)). Kelly, Malamud and Zhou go further and argue that over-parameterised models predict market returns better than simple ones ([JF](https://economics.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2024-01/The%20Journal%20of%20Finance%20-%202023%20-%20KELLY%20-%20The%20Virtue%20of%20Complexity%20in%20Return%20Prediction%20%281%29.pdf)). New signal types include CNN readings of price-chart images ([JF](https://economics.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2023-11/The%20Journal%20of%20Finance%20-%202023%20-%20JIANG%20-%20Re%25E2%2580%2590%20Imag%20in%20ing%20Price%20Trends_0.pdf)) and LLM news representations across 16 markets and 13 languages ([SJTU seminar](https://acem.sjtu.edu.cn/academic/82735.html)), surveyed in Kelly and Xiu's *Financial Machine Learning* ([now publishers](https://nowpublishers.com/article/DownloadSummary/FIN-064)). Formula mining itself moved from genetic programming to reinforcement learning and LLM agents:

Most of these projects run on Qlib against Chinese indices and rarely report results net of realistic costs, so judge them against the ~29,000 data-mined strategies of the flex-mining null. For indicator features, TA-Lib 0.6.x supports NumPy 2 ([PyPI](https://pypi.python.org/pypi/TA-Lib)), whereas pandas-ta's latest release is a beta requiring Python 3.12+ ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/pandas-ta/)) and its GitHub repository returned 404 on 31 October 2026, which makes it a fragile dependency.

Commercial factor sets matter mainly as reference points. MSCI runs more than 70 Barra equity models and now builds crowding, machine-learning, climate and AI factors into them; Axioma, now sold by SimCorp, added an ML "non-linear residual factor" in May 2025 ([SimCorp](https://www.simcorp.com/about-us/news/2025/simcorp-launches-improved-axioma-worldwide-equity-factor-risk-model)) and a short-horizon trading model in January 2026 ([SimCorp](https://www.simcorp.com/about-us/news/2026/simcorp-launches-risk-model-to-support-short-horizon-trading)); Bloomberg's MAC3 computes 3,000+ factors daily ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/company/?p=44191)); and S&P's Alpha Factor Library grew from 500+ signals in 2015 ([S&P](https://press.spglobal.com/2015-12-08-S-P-Capital-IQ-Alpha-Factor-Library-Expands-Global-Coverage-Providing-More-Than-500-Stock-Selection-Signals-on-Over-40-000-Companies-in-90-Countries-Around-the-World)) to 800+ ([S&P product page](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/solutions/products/quantitative-equity-data-alpha-signals)). Only RavenPack's news analytics are within easy reach of an individual, through university WRDS access ([WRDS](https://wrds-www.wharton.upenn.edu/pages/about/data-vendors/ravenpack/)). Because vendor risk models now absorb crowding and ML factors, the line between risk and alpha has blurred: a formula you mine is increasingly likely to sit inside an institution's risk model already. ## China's factor canon lives in broker report series

### Alpha191 needs an errata sheet before it needs a backtest

"Alpha191" is 国泰君安's 数量化专题之九十三 (Quantitative special report No. 93), 《基于短周期价量特征的多因子选股体系》 ("A multi-factor stock-selection system based on short-horizon price-volume features"), cover-dated 15 June 2017 and written by 李辰 and 刘富兵; the often-cited "2017-06-16" is the JoinQuant file name ([GTJA report](https://file.joinquant.com/198f09d54939dbcd27819bb96f67ac15/%E5%9B%BD%E6%B3%B0%E5%90%9B%E5%AE%89-%E6%95%B0%E9%87%8F%E5%8C%96%E4%B8%93%E9%A2%98%E4%B9%8B%E4%B9%9D%E5%8D%81%E4%B8%89%EF%BC%9A%E5%9F%BA%E4%BA%8E%E7%9F%AD%E5%91%A8%E6%9C%9F%E4%BB%B7%E9%87%8F%E7%89%B9%E5%BE%81%E7%9A%84%E5%A4%9A%E5%9B%A0%E5%AD%90%E9%80%89%E8%82%A1%E4%BD%93%E7%B3%BB-2017-06-16.pdf)). It was explicitly a **trading-type (交易型) alpha system**: factors effective "generally within one week," a style-neutral portfolio of 50–60 stocks rebalanced every two days, and **30–50x annual two-sided turnover against about 10x for traditional models**. Its headline — 50.2% annualized excess return over CSI 500 net of costs, a 5.9% maximum drawdown and an information ratio of 4.67 for January 2012 to April 2017 — is an in-sample number from before the crowding described below.

The printed formula appendix contains real errors. The benchmark index's open and close are defined the wrong way round, with "BENCHMARK" misspelled throughout; LOWDAY is defined with HIGHDAY's "maximum" wording; Alpha30 regresses on Fama–French factors the report never supplies; SUMAC is called without its window in Alpha165 and Alpha183; Alpha143 is recursive with no initial value; and the operators differ from WorldQuant's, since RANK is an ascending ordinal sort, WMA uses 0.9^i weights and SMA is an exponential average with α = m/n. Implementations resolve these differently: DolphinDB's `gtja191Alpha` module returns percentile ranks, computes SUMAC as a plain rolling sum "since the original definition was unclear" ([DolphinDB docs](https://docs.dolphindb.cn/zh/modules/gtja191Alpha/191alpha.html)), and implements Alpha143 as a cumulative product of raw returns ([module source](https://github.com/dolphindb/DolphinDBModules/blob/master/gtja191Alpha/1.0.0/src/gtja191Alpha.dos)). Read literally, Alpha143 multiplies its running value by a small return on every up day and collapses toward zero; the intended formula was almost certainly (1 + return) × SELF. Counting the documented errata, roughly 10–15 factors are open to interpretation, so Alpha191 backtests from DolphinDB, JoinQuant and GitHub code are not comparable until those choices are reconciled. The originating team now publishes as 国泰海通 after the merger ([fxbaogao](https://www.fxbaogao.com/detail/5288808)), and a January 2026 paper has carried GTJA-style factors to US stocks ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.06499)).

Other microstructure families originated at 东吴 (the CPV price-volume correlation factor, 2020), 招商 (order-flow alpha), 兴业, 天风 (buy-sell pressure imbalance) and 方正 ([quant-wiki HF page](https://lzw.me/doc/quant-wiki/advanced/%E7%A0%94%E6%8A%A5%E7%B2%BE%E9%80%89/a%E8%82%A1%E9%AB%98%E9%A2%91%E7%A0%94%E6%8A%A5/)), and most of them need Level-2 or tick data, which is the real institutional moat. Curated versions still work: 开源's 聪明钱 2.0, which compares the VWAP of high-"smartness" minute bars with the day's overall VWAP, shows a RankIC (rank correlation with next-period returns) of −6.4% and an IR of 2.09 over July 2021 to April 2026, and version 3.0, released on 28 August 2026 with tick and snapshot inputs, reaches a RankIC of −7.21%, a 27.59% annualized long-short return and an IR of 3.21 ([Sina](https://finance.sina.cn/2026-08-29/detail-inipyfhv5524506.d.html)). Two series bridge to academia — 华安's 学海拾珠 and 招商's 琢璞 translate and replicate academic papers — and continuity is a growing risk: 东方's 朱剑涛, who led about 100 factor reports, moved to 光大保德信基金 in 2024 ([Sina](https://finance.sina.cn/2026-06-29/detail-inieznpi0346179.d.html)), while 华泰's 林晓明 and 何康 went to private funds and 中信建投's chief quant 丁鲁明 left in July 2025 ([Sina](https://finance.sina.cn/2026-10-09/detail-iniuqywq1106497.d.html)). Check the author and date before treating any series' latest report as the house view.

### Academic China models set the stricter benchmark

Liu, Stambaugh and Yuan's "Size and Value in China" (JFE 2019) is the foundational A-share factor model ([working paper](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/Size-and-Value-in-China.pdf)). It drops the smallest 30% of firms because they are priced as reverse-merger shells — 83% of reverse mergers used such shells, and about 40% of a typical bottom-30% stock's value was shell value — and it measures value by earnings-to-price, which subsumes book-to-market. A plain Fama–French three-factor model leaves a 17% annual alpha on the earnings-to-price factor, and CH-3 explains 8 of 10 CAPM anomalies against 3 for FF-3; CH-4 adds an abnormal-turnover sentiment factor in a market where individuals owned 88% of the free float. The official CH-3/CH-4 files stop at December 2021, with a monthly CH-3 series updated externally ([Stambaugh](https://fnce.wharton.upenn.edu/profile/stambaug); [Mingshi](https://en.mingshiim.com/database)).

Three replications set the stricter benchmark. Li, Liu, Liu and Wei (Management Science 2024) test **469 anomalies** with main-board breakpoints and value weights and find that **83.37% show no significant raw spread, 84.22% no CAPM alpha and 86.99% no FF3 alpha**, with the q-factor model fitting best after 2007 ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v70y2024i8p5066-5090.html)). Jansen, Swinkels and Zhou (2021) test 32 anomalies over 2000–2019 with open data: value, risk and trading anomalies carry over, size, quality and past-return effects are much weaker except residual momentum and reversal, and their first China tests find strong return seasonality ([Erasmus](https://pure.eur.nl/en/publications/anomalies-in-the-china-a-share-market/)). Leippold, Wang and Zhou (JFE 2022) find liquidity, not price trend, to be the most important ML predictor, with retail-driven short-horizon predictability concentrated in small stocks and fundamentals mattering more than in the US ([SFI](https://www.sfi.ch/en/publications/machine-learning-in-the-chinese-stock-market)). Together these studies identify the durable A-share effects as earnings-yield value, turnover and illiquidity (including abnormal-turnover sentiment), short-term and residual reversal, volatility and lottery effects, and residual momentum, while raw momentum, investment and quality add little once CH-3 is in the model; equal-weighted, all-board sorts overstate tradable alpha.

## Net of costs, the average anomaly earns four basis points a month

Two decades of meta-research converge on a narrow conclusion: most published anomalies are statistically real in their samples, and little of that survives as exploitable return. Harvey, Liu and Zhu's catalogue of 316 factors set the modern t > 3.0 hurdle ([NBER](https://nber.org/papers/w20592)), and the census reached 382 factors by January 2019 ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3341728)). Andrew Chen counters that the same false-discovery arithmetic implies most findings are true, with the pessimism coming from equating "insignificant" with "false" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.15365)). The joint tests settle the practical question. Green, Hand and Zhang found only about 12 of 94 characteristics independently predicted non-microcap US returns over 1980–2014, and **only 2 after 2003** ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v30y2017i12p4389-4436..html)). The dimension-reduction literature compresses the zoo to JKP's 13 themes, to about 15 factors whose styles persist while the best factor for each style changes ([Swade et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4605976)), to the few survivors of a 150-factor library ([Feng, Giglio & Xiu](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25481.pdf)), or to a handful of robust factors among 51 tested across 2.25 quadrillion candidate models ([Bryzgalova, Huang & Julliard](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/julliard/papers/BSftFT.pdf)). By extension, a 101- or 191-formula price-volume library most likely spans a few themes — reversal, volatility, turnover and volume–price correlation — in many guises, and mapping each formula to its theme is worth more than adding formulas.

Decay and costs shrink what remains. McLean and Pontiff's 97 predictors earn **26% less out of sample and 58% less after publication** ([paper](https://www.gwern.net/doc/economics/2016-mclean.pdf)), although across 241 anomalies in 39 markets only the US shows a significant post-publication decline ([Jacobs & Müller](https://fin.wiwi.uni-due.de/forschung/publikationen/anomalies-across-the-globe-once-public-no-longer-existent-14591/)). Chen, Lopez-Lira and Zimmermann find peer-reviewed predictors decaying about as much as data-mined ones, with roughly half the predictability remaining, and risk-based predictors doing no better than mined benchmarks ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10317)). The decisive number comes from Chen and Velikov's study of 204 anomalies: net of spreads, post-publication decay and the post-2000s trading environment, the average anomaly earns **4 basis points a month**, the strongest at most 10 bps and a combination about 20 bps, before price impact ([JFQA](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/zeroing-in-on-the-expected-returns-of-anomalies/945133D5A3ECEEAF466AEE91551FD225)). Novy-Marx and Velikov show that strategies with one-sided monthly turnover below 50% keep significant net spreads when built to economize on trading, with hold-until-exit bands the most effective simple fix ([NBER](https://nber.org/papers/w20721)), and their toolkit has a Python port ([AssayingAnomalies](https://pypi.org/project/AssayingAnomalies/1.3.2/)). WorldQuant's 4,000-alpha evidence that returns do not depend on turnover reflects institutional execution, not retail, and Alpha191's design shows why that matters: at 30–50x two-sided turnover, the 0.05% A-share stamp duty on sales alone costs **0.75–1.25% of capital a year**, before commissions, spreads and market impact.

Two time-series results offer a modest use for factor knowledge. The average factor earns **1 bp a month after a losing year and 53 bps after a winning year**, so stock momentum largely aggregates factor momentum ([Ehsani & Linnainmaa](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25551)), and forecasting the first five principal components of anomaly returns, which explain 60% of their variation, improves on static factor investing ([Haddad, Kozak & Santosh](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w26708/w26708.pdf)). The robust reading is a slow tilt toward recently strong factor themes, not aggressive timing. López de Prado's *Causal Factor Investing* adds the deepest critique: without a causal mechanism, associational factor findings are likely false because of backtest overfitting and specification choices ([RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/b/cup/cbooks/9781009397292.html)).

The table carries three lessons. The pre-2017 size premium was partly a policy artifact — IPO constraints made tiny listed firms valuable as shells — and regulation erased it within a year. Short-horizon price-volume and high-frequency factors, Alpha191's territory, were mined intensively from 2016, decayed visibly by 2020 and then became crowding risk, driving the unwinds of late 2021, January–February 2024 and July 2026, when by one industry account factors that had earned excess return "became sources of drawdown". And rules removed inputs and positions outright between 2024 and 2025, from northbound flow data to short legs to high-frequency order rates. A-share factor alpha clearly still exists — 2025 was a banner year, and curated microstructure factors keep strong rank correlations — but it is an institutional business that needs Level-2 data, fast execution, crowding-aware risk models and the capacity to absorb drawdowns. A retail account running the same public price-volume factors inherits the crowding risk without the execution edge, which is the strongest argument for the approaches below.

## Slow risk premia suit individuals; speed-based edges do not

Individuals have structural advantages that institutions lack — no capacity constraints, no benchmark or career risk, the freedom to sit through multi-year flat periods, tax-advantaged accounts and exchange-traded leverage through futures — and structural handicaps in retail borrowing fees, wide spreads and slower execution. The administrative evidence on active retail trading is uniformly negative. Barber and Odean's most active US households earned 11.4% a year against the market's 17.9% ([paper](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers%20current%20versions/individual_investor_performance_final.pdf)); fewer than 1% of Taiwanese day traders were predictably profitable net of fees ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finmar/v18y2014icp1-24.html)); and among Brazilian mini-index futures day traders who persisted beyond 300 sessions, **only 1.1% earned more than the minimum wage** ([EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:fgv:eesptd:525)). FINRA's investor page on day trading, read on 31 October 2026, describes intraday margin with a $2,000 minimum and no longer mentions the $25,000 pattern-day-trader threshold ([FINRA](https://www.finra.org/investors/investing/investment-products/stocks/day-trading)); given the evidence above, a lower barrier is more likely to raise retail losses than to create an edge. The approaches below are therefore judged on whether their edge survives publication, costs and investor behaviour.

### Trend following pays in crises, not in Sharpe ratios

Trend following has the longest and most consistent evidence base of any approach here. AQR's century-long simulation combines 1-, 3- and 12-month time-series momentum across 67 futures and forwards, sizes each market to equal volatility, targets 10% portfolio volatility, rebalances monthly and deducts fees and inflated historical costs; every lookback was positive in every decade from 1880 to 2016, and the strategy made money in **8 of the 10 largest 60/40 drawdowns**, which lasted about 15 months on average and gave trends time to form ([Hurst, Ooi & Pedersen](https://fairmodel.econ.yale.edu/ec439/hurst.pdf)). Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen found 12-month time-series momentum profitable in all 58 liquid futures they studied over 1985–2009 ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2089463)). The sceptics attack the statistics, not the payoff shape: Huang, Li, Wang and Zhou show the pooled t-statistic fails bootstrap critical values and that the strategy does about as well as one built on historical mean returns ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v135y2020i3p774-794.html)), and Kim, Tse and Wald report that without volatility scaling the monthly alpha falls from 1.27% to 0.41% ([Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/deconstructing-the-time-series-momentum-strategy/)). The case for trend therefore rests on crisis convexity and diversification, not on a large standalone Sharpe ratio.

Realized industry returns are modest and lumpy. The SG Trend Index returned +20.9% in 2008 and +27.4% in 2022 while global equities fell 42.2% and 18.4%, then suffered a −20.4% drawdown from May 2024 to May 2025, its second-worst since 2000, as tariff whipsaws reversed trends; it has had 16 drawdowns larger than 10% since 2000, about one every 18–24 months ([Cambridge Associates](https://www.cambridgeassociates.com/en-eu/insight/does-trend-followings-recent-struggle-signal-that-the-strategy-is-structurally-broken/)). It finished 2025 up 2.39% ([TTU](https://www.toptradersunplugged.com/trend-following-performance-report-december-2025/)), stood **up 15.80% for 2026 by September** and has compounded **5.75% a year since 2000 with a 20.61% maximum drawdown**, against 8.23% and 50.95% for the S&P 500 total return ([TTU](https://www.toptradersunplugged.com/trend-following-performance-report-september-2026/)); BarclayHedge's estimate put it at +14.92% for the year on 31 October 2026 ([BarclayHedge](https://portal.barclayhedge.com/cgi-bin/indices/displayHfIndex.cgi?indexCat=SG-Prime-Services-Indices&indexName=SG-Trend-Index)). AQR attributes the weak 2010s to fewer large market moves rather than a structural break ([AQR](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/White-Papers/You-Cant-Always-Trend-When-You-Want)), and the 2020–2026 record fits that reading: gains in the slow 2022 inflation shock, losses in the V-shaped reversals of 2024–2025, recovery in 2026.

Individuals have real advantages here, because liquid futures impose no capacity limit, monthly signals keep turnover low and futures provide exchange-margined leverage; the binding constraint is capital granularity. Robert Carver, formerly a portfolio manager at the hedge fund AHL, recommends at least $100,000 for a diversified futures system ([Harriman House](https://www.harriman-house.com/advancedfuturestradingstrat)), and his open-source pysystemtrade now lives under the pst-group organisation, releasing 1.8.3 on 30 September 2026 ([GitHub](https://github.com/pst-group/pysystemtrade)). Below that size, managed-futures ETFs are the practical route. KraneShares' KMLM holds 22 commodity, currency and bond futures for a 0.90% fee and returned +19.23% in 2026 to September but only +0.41% a year over three years ([KraneShares](https://kraneshares.com/kmlm/)), while DBMF replicates the SG CTA Index from liquid futures for about 0.85% ([comparison](https://pictureperfectportfolios.com/whats-the-best-managed-futures-etf-dbmf-vs-kmlm-vs-cta/)). The gap between KMLM's three-year and current-year numbers argues for combining replication methods rather than betting on one vendor's model.

### Allocation rules buy shallower drawdowns with lower returns

Published tactical asset allocation rules mostly kept their drawdown protection out of sample but lost much of their return:

| Rule | In-sample or pre-publication | Out of sample |
|---|---|---|
| Faber GTAA (10-month moving-average filter, five asset classes) | 1972–2005: 11.7% CAGR, Sharpe 0.81 | 2006–March 2025: 6.05% CAGR, Sharpe 0.68 ([Concretum](https://concretumgroup.substack.com/p/global-tactical-asset-allocation)) |
| Antonacci GEM (dual momentum) | Beat the S&P 500 by 5.1 points a year, 1997–2013 | Trailed it by 5.4 points a year, 2014–2026 ([replication](https://github.com/AlgoStrategyLab/dual-momentum)); 8.4% CAGR against 13.6% for SPY and 9.1% for a static 60/40 ([Quant for Free](https://quant4free.com/analysis/dual-momentum/)) |
| Adaptive Asset Allocation (top-5 momentum, minimum-variance weights) | 1996–2014: Sharpe 1.33 | 2015–2023: Sharpe 0.57 ([FOSS Trading](https://www.r-bloggers.com/2024/01/adaptive-asset-allocation-extended/amp/)) |

The pattern matches the general rule from Falck, Rej and Thesmar that Sharpe ratios roughly halve after publication and that signal complexity predicts how much they decay ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.01380)) — a warning against heavily parameterized variants such as Keller's breadth-momentum family, with its tuned breadth parameters and "canary" asset sets ([CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/strategic-allocation/multi-class-momentum-portfolio-with-canary-crash-protection/)). Part of the 2014–2026 shortfall reflects a US mega-cap bull market punctuated by fast crashes, the worst environment for 10–12-month filters. The realistic job of these rules is cutting drawdowns so that investors stay invested, not beating equities, and because monthly switching realizes taxable gains they belong in tax-advantaged accounts where possible.

Volatility targeting is the risk-control tool with the best evidence, provided its claims stay modest. Across more than 60 assets from 1926 to 2017 it raises Sharpe ratios for equities and credit, has negligible effect for bonds, currencies and commodities, and reduces extreme returns everywhere ([Harvey et al. via Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/volatility-targeting-improves-risk-adjusted-returns/)). The stronger claim that volatility management creates alpha ([Moreira & Muir](https://www.nber.org/papers/22208)) fails in real time, where implementations were worse for **72 of 103 equity strategies** ([Cederburg et al.](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/COWY.pdf)). Risk parity exposed its hidden assumption in 2022, when stocks and bonds fell together: Bridgewater's All Weather lost 22% ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/04/23/lackluster-returns-ray-dalio-investing-strategy-investors-pull-billions/)) and the RPAR ETF more than 32% from its peak ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2022-09-28/-perfect-storm-for-risk-parity-has-etf-posting-record-32-drop)), while the Permanent Portfolio lost only 5.5% ([CAIA](https://caia.org/node/7312)). Daily-reset leveraged ETFs are a poor substitute for futures leverage; TQQQ's 2022 drawdown reached −81% against −35% for an unleveraged Nasdaq-100 fund ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/should-investors-use-leveraged-etfs-or-avoid-them-altogether); [Ryan O'Connell](https://ryanoconnellfinance.com/leveraged-etfs/)).

### Option writing harvests a small premium; option buying funds dealers

Systematic index option writing has delivered equity-like returns along a smoother path. By Cboe's figures, the BXM buy-write index returned about **8.5% a year since 1986 against 11.1% for the S&P 500**, with volatility of 10.7% against 15.2% and a maximum drawdown of −35.8% against −50.9% ([Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/benchmark-indices-series-income-generation-and-smoother-returns-with-cboes-bxm-bxmd-put-and-cmbo-indices)). AQR's decomposition explains why: a covered call combines equity exposure, which carries most of the risk and return, a short-volatility position with a Sharpe ratio near 1.0 but under 10% of the risk, and an uncompensated short "equity reversal" timing bet worth about a quarter of the risk, and hedging out that third exposure improves the result ([Israelov & Nielsen](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/Covered-Calls-Uncovered)). Buying protection fares worse: with a normal volatility premium puts can deepen drawdowns, and a static mix of 36.5% S&P 500 and 63.5% cash matched the excess return of Cboe's 5% put-protection index ([AQR](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/White-Papers/Pathetic-Protection-The-Elusive-Benefits-of-Protective-Puts)).

The failure modes are leverage and short-dated speculation. On 5 February 2018 the VIX jumped about 20 points, a one-day record, and the XIV short-volatility note lost most of its value and was terminated ([BIS](https://www.bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt1803t.htm)). Same-day-expiry (0DTE) options reached **59% of SPX volume in 2025** ([Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/the-state-of-the-options-industry-2025)) and a record 62.4% in August 2025, when retail traders supplied an estimated 53% of that flow ([Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/spx-0-dte-options-jump-to-record-62-share-in-august)); retail 0DTE traders lost about $358,000 a day after daily expirations began in May 2022 ([WealthManagement](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/equities/retail-investors-are-in-love-with-same-day-options)), and across options generally retail investors favour cheap weekly contracts with an average bid-ask spread of **12.6%** and lose money on average ([Bryzgalova, Pavlova & Sikorskaya](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/id/eprint/2827/)). The tail-hedging debate continues — Universa reported a 3,612% return in the 2020 crash while AQR-side research favours trend following as the cheaper crisis diversifier ([BNN Bloomberg](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/why-one-firm-s-3-612-return-is-drawing-the-ire-of-hedge-funds-1.1903852)) — but for individuals the conclusion is clear: put-writing and covered calls on broad indices, sized as an equity substitute, are viable, while leveraged short-volatility products and short-dated option buying are not.

### Carry pays until liquidity breaks, and crypto carry prices venue risk

Carry is one of the broadest documented premia. Koijen, Moskowitz, Pedersen and Vrugt find that carry predicts returns across asset classes, with Sharpe ratios of about **0.7 within an asset class and 1.1 for a diversified portfolio**, but carry strategies lose together in global recessions and liquidity crunches ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w19325)); the August 2024 yen carry unwind erased the year's carry gains within days ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-08-06/jpmorgan-says-carry-trade-unwind-is-only-half-complete)). Individuals are better served by carry as one signal inside a diversified futures system than by a concentrated currency-carry book.

Crypto offers the largest retail-accessible spreads. BIS research finds Bitcoin and Ether futures basis — long spot, short futures — reaching **about 60% a year**, driven by leveraged retail demand and scarce arbitrage capital, with high carry predicting crashes ([BIS](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1087.htm)). Perpetual-futures funding arbitrage on Bitcoin earns a Sharpe ratio of about **1.8 at retail costs** and up to 3.5 for fee-free market makers, but the deviations "diminish over time" ([He, Manela, Ross & von Wachter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.06888v6)). The risk is the venue rather than market beta. FTX filed for bankruptcy nine days after Alameda's balance sheet leaked ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bankruptcy_of_FTX)), and on 10 October 2025 more than $19 billion was liquidated across about 1.6 million accounts while the USDe stablecoin traded near $0.65 on Binance and on-chain pools held about $1; exchanges' auto-deleveraging (ADL) then closed profitable positions on the winning side, which can strip a hedged trader's protective leg ([CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/learn/october-10-crypto-crash-explained)). How much of the spread remains in 2026 could not be established from the evidence gathered. Grid and DCA (dollar-cost averaging) bots showed no evidence of edge — a grid is short volatility in disguise and DCA is a deployment schedule — and their documented extra risk is operational: leaked 3Commas API keys let attackers trade victims' accounts, costing about $14.9 million in November 2022 alone ([ForkLog/HAPI](https://forklog.com/en/news/api-key-leaks-and-exchange-inaction-a-hapi-analysis-of-the-3commas-incident)). API keys should be trade-only, with withdrawals disabled.

### Short-horizon, calendar and LLM edges have mostly been competed away

Short-horizon equity arbitrage has decayed into an institutional scale game. Gatev, Goetzmann and Rouwenhorst's distance-based pairs trading earned up to about 11% a year for top pairs over 1962–2002 ([NBER](https://nber.org/papers/w7032)), but profitability then declined and, after commissions, market impact and short-selling fees, became "much more modest," concentrated in turbulent periods ([Do & Faff](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/41722/)). Khandani and Lo show a simple contrarian strategy's average daily return falling to **0.13% by 2006** as capital crowded in, before an unleveraged loss of about 12 daily standard deviations in August 2007 ([NBER](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w14465/w14465.pdf)), and a 2016–2026 test of Connors' RSI(2) dip-buying rule on the S&P 500 earned about 1.7% a year against 13.5% for buy-and-hold ([Backtest](https://backtest.substack.com/p/the-2-period-rsi-a-simple-system)).

Calendar and event effects mostly faded after publication. The return in the 24 hours before scheduled Fed announcements once accounted for more than 80% of the US equity premium over 1994–2011 ([NY Fed](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:fip:fednsr:512)) but essentially disappeared after 2015 ([Kurov et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v40y2021ics1544612320315956.html)). Post-earnings drift has been absent in large stocks since 2006 ([Martineau](https://cfr.ivo-welch.info/published/papers/martineau2021rest.pdf)), and the 2025 claims of its revival hinge on microcaps — one replication gives t = 2.18 with all stocks but 1.43 without them ([UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/is-post-earnings-announcement-drift-a-thing-again/)). S&P 500 addition returns shrank from about 7.5% in the 1990s to under 1% in the last decade ([Greenwood & Sammon](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w30748/w30748.pdf)); NightShares' overnight-return ETF closed in 2023 after returning −6.9% while the S&P 500 gained 22% ([WealthManagement](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/etfs/night-effect-etfs-shut-down-overnight-stock-returns-elusive)); and McConnell and Xu's finding that investors were rewarded for market risk only at turns of the month covers 1926–2005 ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/ufajxx/v64y2008i2p49-64.html)), with no cost-adjusted post-2005 confirmation established here. What survives tends to be payment for bearing risk: merger arbitrage earned about 4% a year after costs over 1963–1998 with a crash profile like writing index puts ([Mitchell & Pulvino](https://www.johnhcochrane.com/s/mitchell-pulvino-characteristics-of-risk-and-return-JF.pdf)), and it is best held through diversified funds.

Machine-learning and LLM signals share the same weaknesses. GPT-4 readings of headlines predicted post-announcement drift, especially in small stocks and on negative news, but strategy returns decline as LLM adoption rises ([Lopez-Lira & Tang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07619)); LLM backtests also suffer look-ahead bias and a larger "distraction effect," in which the model's general knowledge of a firm distorts its sentiment reading, which anonymized headlines are proposed to fix ([Glasserman & Lin](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17322)). With daily data on a few dozen assets, an individual sits in the overfitting regime rather than the virtue-of-complexity regime, so ML is more useful for volatility forecasting, data cleaning and execution than as a standalone alpha source.

### How the global approaches compare

Before trusting any backtest, apply base-rate haircuts. Across 215 bank "alternative beta" products the median Sharpe ratio fell from 1.20 in backtests to **0.31 live, a 73% decline**, and the most complex products deteriorated about 30% more ([Suhonen et al. via Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/beware-of-backtest-overfitting/)); among 888 Quantopian algorithms, backtest Sharpe ratios were a poor guide to live results ([Wiecki et al.](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/208/All%20that%20Glitters%20Is%20Not%20Gold%20-%20Comparing%20Backtest%20and%20Out-of-Sample%20Performance%20on%20a%20Large%20Cohort%20o.pdf)); and the probability of backtest overfitting can be estimated with combinatorially symmetric cross-validation and corrected with the deflated Sharpe ratio ([Bailey et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253)). A workable protocol for an individual is to count every variant tried, require t > 3 or deflate the Sharpe ratio, prefer rules with few parameters that work across many markets, halve the backtest Sharpe ratio, and run small for a year before scaling.

## China's retail edges are structural, policy-made and short-lived

The best account-level evidence on Chinese retail traders comes from Jones, Shi, Zhang and Zhang (JFQA, February 2025), who use complete Shanghai Stock Exchange account data for 2016–2019. Large retail accounts predict returns, trade against recent moves and use public news, while small accounts cannot forecast returns, chase daily momentum and show gambling preferences ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/jfinqa/v60y2025i1p68-104_3.html); [Tsinghua summary](https://eng.pbcsf.tsinghua.edu.cn/info/1072/2762.htm)); retail investors supply about 85% of exchange volume ([ABFER](https://abfer.org/media/abfer-events-2022/annual-conference/papers-investfin/AC22P3020_Understanding-Retail-Investors_Evidence-from-China.pdf)) in a market of about 250 million investors at end-2025 ([财联社](https://www.cls.cn/detail/2086830)). The lesson for a retail quant is not to compete with faster participants on the same short-horizon signals. The real opportunities are structural features of China's market — convertible bonds, subscription lotteries, repo calendars and commodity-futures access — each created and continually reshaped by rules.

### Convertible-bond 双低 rotation has the best record and the worst setup

The convertible-bond (CB) rulebook was reset in 2022: SSE listing-day price limits became +57.3% and −43.3% with ±20% daily limits thereafter, under trading rules effective 1 August 2022 ([每经](https://www.nbd.com.cn/articles/2022-06-19/2328956.html); [中证网](https://www.cs.com.cn/xwzx/hg/202209/t20220902_6295712.html)), and from 18 June 2022 new individual investors have needed two years of trading experience and 100,000 yuan (10万元) in assets. The 双低 ("double-low") strategy ranks bonds by price plus 100 × conversion premium and rotates monthly into the cheapest. Wind's 双低 index **returned 124% from 2018 to 2024, or 13.72% a year with 11.43% volatility**, including +15.53% in 2024 with an 8.73% maximum drawdown; over 2018–2024 the underlying stocks contributed about 54% of the return and valuation about 41%, while the bond floor and conversion-price resets (下修) added about 2% each ([申万宏源 via Sina](https://finance.sina.cn/bond/zsyj/2025-01-09/detail-ineekuyn9306337.d.html)).

The edge was cheap equity optionality plus valuation expansion, not the bond floor — and conditions have since turned against every one of those drivers. In 2025 the number of CBs fell by 116 to 413 and outstanding value by 1,759.74亿元 to 5,577.58亿元; **77% of the 173 exits came through forced redemption (强赎)**, and median prices ended above 131元 against par of 100元, with median conversion premiums above 32% ([证券时报](https://www.stcn.com/article/detail/3554514.html)). The CSI convertible-bond index closed above 500 on 6 January 2026 for the first time in ten years ([财联社](https://www.cls.cn/detail/2249517)), but by 15 October it had fallen to 446.05, more than 17% below its high, with valuations at the **94th–97th percentile since 2017**; none of 89 CB funds gained over the preceding month, and only 10 were up over one year ([财联社](https://www.cls.cn/detail/2505885)). Defaults have become real. 搜特转债 (2023) was the first substantive default, with stock and bond delisted without a consolidation period ([同花顺](https://news.10jqka.com.cn/20260709/c678074991.shtml)); 龙大转债 became 2026's first default in July; and on 19 October 2026 *ST岭南 triggered delisting for trading below face value, joining several issuers whose stock and bond were delisted together (双退). Defaulted bonds are under 2% of all CBs ever issued, but holders rarely recover in full ([同花顺](https://news.10jqka.com.cn/20261019/c680808637.shtml)).

A 双低 basket now holds either expensive equity-like bonds or cheap bonds that carry real credit and delisting risk, so expect lower and more credit-sensitive returns than in 2018–2024, filter on issuer credit, a stock price above 1元 and ST status, and track redemption and reset countdowns. 集思录 (Jisilu) refreshes its CB table every 30 seconds and flags announced redemptions and reset triggers ([集思录](https://www.jisilu.cn/data/cbnew/)).

### Subscription lotteries keep a positive but binary expectancy

New-share subscription (打新) recovered strongly after the 2024 slowdown. In 2025, 116 A-share IPOs raised 1,317.71亿元, against 100 raising 673.53亿元 in 2024, and 摩尔线程 and 沐曦 rose 425% and 693% on their first days ([证券市场周刊](https://static.weeklyonstock.com/26/0107/wd171329.html)). Deloitte counted an average first-day gain of about **257% with no IPO closing below its issue price** in 2025, at average allotment ratios around 0.03% ([第一财经](https://www.yicai.com/news/102967170.html); [36氪](https://www.36kr.com/p/3618845063841033)), and first-half 2026 first-day gains averaged about 280% ([新华财经](https://www.cnfin.com/yw-lb/detail/20260630/4433726_1.html)). Because subscription quota depends on average Shanghai or Shenzhen stock holdings ([CSDN](https://damodev.csdn.net/6a438bc210ee7a33f2850a6e.html)), the payoff is a lottery: one 雪球 (Xueqiu) investor with quota for 16,000 Shanghai and 10,000 Shenzhen shares won nothing in 2025, while a single winning lot of 沐曦 or 摩尔线程 was worth about 363,000 or 243,000 yuan ([雪球](https://www.xueqiu.com/1910558321/368700468)). IPO subscription is best treated as a free option attached to stock holdings you would own anyway, sold on listing.

CB subscription is smaller still. The 39 CBs listed in 2026 by 5 August averaged a +52.56% first day, with 27 closing at the +57.3% cap, but the average online allotment ratio was **0.0031%** ([南方财经](https://m.sfccn.com/2026/8-6/0OMDE0MDRfMjIwMDg0OQ.html)). That works out to about 0.016元 of expected gain per 1,000元 lot applied for; an account applying for 1,000 lots per issue (caps vary by issue) can expect roughly 16元 per issue, or 600–700元 across the 39 issues listed by 5 August — positive, but trivial.

Futures and options access is gated by asset thresholds:

The performance record is the strongest of any Chinese private-fund strategy recently. In 2024 CTA funds averaged **12.18%, the best of five private-fund strategies**, with trend followers beating cross-sectional and fundamental CTAs ([中证网](https://www.cs.com.cn/tzjj/smjj/202501/t20250110_6467358.html)). Through 30 September 2026, 1,344 CTA funds averaged **+6.39%**, with 72% positive, against +2.37% for all 10,818 private securities funds, and in September CTA was the only positive sub-strategy ([上证报 via 新浪](https://finance.sina.cn/2026-10-18/detail-inivrkxi6734055.d.html)). The volatility regime helps: the Nanhua commodity index's volatility averaged above 18% in 2025–2026, against about 13% in 2023–2024 ([财联社](https://www.cls.cn/detail/2514349)).

Three caveats apply. Exchanges change the rules mid-squeeze: during the 2025–2026 silver squeeze the Shanghai Futures Exchange raised silver's daily limits to 16% and margins to 18% and capped daily new positions at 7,000 lots per contract, its fourth tightening since December 2025 ([第一财经](https://www.yicai.com/news/102997700.html)), so leveraged trend systems must survive gaps. Private-fund averages carry survivorship bias and differing fees, so they overstate what a retail replication would earn. And no academic study of time-series momentum in Chinese commodity futures was found. A vn.py/CTP trend system can be built, but it falls under the futures program-trading reporting rules described below.

### ETF rotation has a vast menu but no verified record

The menu that rotation strategies need now exists: by end-2025 China had 1,381 ETFs worth about 6.02万亿元, and cross-border ETFs passed 1万亿元 in January 2026 ([同花顺](https://news.10jqka.com.cn/20260119/c674104885.shtml); [每经](https://www.nbd.com.cn/articles/2026-01-13/4217433.html)). Execution changed on 6 July 2026, when Shanghai-listed funds began closing through a 14:57–15:00 call auction and post-close fixed-price trading was extended to all A-shares and ETFs ([同花顺](https://news.10jqka.com.cn/20260701/c677870245.shtml)), so a daily rotation can now trade at the official close, and backtests spanning that date should model the change. The evidence, however, is thin. This research found no cost-inclusive performance record for 二八轮动 (rotating between large-cap and small-cap indices), sector or cross-border rotation, or ETF grid trading, and could not confirm which ETF categories permit same-day round trips under the 2026 rules; check the exchanges' rules before building intraday ETF strategies.

Cross-border and commodity fund premiums are traps rather than arbitrage. 国投白银LOF, a silver fund, traded at a **68.19% premium to net asset value** on 24 December 2025 before falling limit-down two days running, and subscribing to sell on the exchange faced a 100元 daily subscription cap and two-day settlement ([第一财经](https://www.yicai.com/news/102997700.html)). Nearly 20 listed open-ended funds (LOFs) hit limit-up on premiums in late 2025 ([每经](https://www.nbd.com.cn/articles/2026-01-08/4212802.html)), and 易方达's Nasdaq ETF closed about 10% above its indicative value in August 2026 and was suspended ([SZSE notice](https://disc.static.szse.cn/disc/disk03/finalpage/2026-08-18/02c3552a-c6d2-41c6-adfc-0b9ed3271bca.PDF)), even though approved QDII quota had reached US$183 billion ([同花顺](https://news.10jqka.com.cn/20260901/c679463485.shtml)). Without shorting, a premium trade is a directional bet that the premium will shrink. The practical rules are to base momentum signals on net asset value rather than price, cap the premium paid on entry, and rotate into a sister ETF on the same index that trades near net asset value.

Treasury reverse repo (国债逆回购) is the cleanest small edge for idle cash. The one-day Shanghai rate once hit 32% intraday in March 2017 ([每经](https://www.nbd.com.cn/articles/2017-03-30/1090088.html)); since May 2017 interest accrues for the actual days funds are used, so a one-day repo placed before a weekend or holiday earns several days' interest ([每经](https://www.nbd.com.cn/articles/2017-05-31/1112408.html)); and fees were 1元 per 100,000元 per day in 2020 ([杭州网](https://hznews.hangzhou.com.cn/jingji/content/2020-06/29/content_7764050.htm)). How large quarter-end spikes were in 2024–2026 is unknown, so treat it as a cash overlay rather than a strategy.

### Micro-caps, dividend low-vol and snowballs price crowded premia

Micro-cap rotation has a spectacular long-run record punctuated by crowding crashes. The Wind micro-cap index **rose 508% over the ten years to June 2026**, then fell 23.68% from 12 May to 13 July 2026 as trading moved to large-cap tech ([上证报](https://paper.cnstock.com/html/2026-07/19/content_2246198.htm)); the CSI 2000 fell 32.84% between 2 January and 5 February 2024 ([复旦](https://fddi.fudan.edu.cn/_t2515/2b/23/c21253a666403/page.htm)), and micro-caps dropped more than 12% in late December 2024 around a list of stocks at risk of delisting ([同花顺](https://stock.10jqka.com.cn/20241229/c664989705.shtml)). This is a liquidity and crowding premium with recurring 20–35% crashes, and it needs explicit crash rules on turnover, crowding and the large-cap/micro-cap spread, plus delisting-risk exclusions. The CSI dividend low-volatility index (中证红利低波, H30269), a low-turnover, yield-weighted basket of 50 stocks, has the opposite profile: total returns of +18.45% in 2019, +1.99% in 2020, +16.34% in 2021, +3.46% in 2022, +12.23% in 2023 and +24.82% in 2024, then +5.57% in 2025 and −1.43% in 2026 to 13 August, with a worst drawdown of −17.54% in 2020 ([雪球 analysis](https://www.xueqiu.com/9607639944/405174700)). It is a reasonable low-turnover core, though its premium has stalled since 2025.

Snowball structured notes (雪球) are the cautionary case: short put options with a knock-out feature. In January 2024 about 1,380亿元 of snowballs referenced the CSI 500 and 920亿元 the CSI 1000, typically with knock-in levels at 70–80% of the starting index; one 2-million-yuan CSI 500 snowball lost all its principal and coupons after the index fell 26.83%, an analyst estimated that classic structures had a 78.5% chance of profit but an average absolute return of only 6–8%, and dealers unwinding hedges pushed CSI 500 and CSI 1000 futures to limit-down on 22 January ([华尔街见闻](https://wallstreetcn.com/articles/3707839)). For limit-up chasing (打板), following the exchange's 龙虎榜 trading-desk disclosures and intraday round-tripping against a base position (底仓做T), no rigorous evidence was found, and the Jones et al. results describe exactly the losing small-account momentum cohort. The widening of ST-stock price limits from ±5% to ±10% on 6 July 2026 also makes earlier limit-up calibrations on those names stale ([同花顺](https://news.10jqka.com.cn/20260701/c677870245.shtml)).

### Shorting is shut, and every API strategy must be reported first

Two trading constraints bind every retail design. Stamp duty has been 0.05%, paid by the seller, since 28 August 2023 ([华尔街见闻](https://wallstreetcn.com/articles/3696465)). Securities lending for short sales (转融券) has been suspended since 11 July 2024, short-sale margin rose to 100% (120% for private funds) on 22 July 2024, and the short balance shrank to about 0.05% of free-float value ([CSRC](https://www.csrc.gov.cn/csrc/c100028/c7493852/content.shtml)). With T+1 stock settlement and no practical single-stock shorting, retail long-short and market-neutral equity strategies are infeasible, and hedging is possible only through index futures or options behind 50万元 thresholds.

Program-trading regulation now reaches individuals. "No program trading without a prior report" has applied since 9 October 2023, and reports must name the software, its version and developer, the strategy type and the maximum order rates ([华尔街见闻](https://wallstreetcn.com/articles/3696980)). The framework built up in stages:

| Effective | Rule | Key content |
|---|---|---|
| 9 October 2023 | Exchange reporting notices | Report before trading; software, strategy and order-rate details |
| 8 October 2024 | CSRC program-trading provisions | The securities market's first dedicated rulebook ([同花顺](https://news.10jqka.com.cn/20240516/c657883396.shtml)) |
| 7 July 2025 | Exchange implementation rules | HFT is ≥300 orders and cancels per second or ≥20,000 per day per account; four types of abnormal trading monitored ([SSE](https://www.sse.com.cn/aboutus/mediacenter/hotandd/c/c_20250403_10776805.shtml)) |

During the 2024 trial of the abnormal-trading indicators, triggers came mainly from quant private funds and broker proprietary desks, while small and medium individual investors were "basically unaffected" ([SSE](https://www.sse.com.cn/aboutus/mediacenter/hotandd/c/c_20250403_10776805.shtml)). On the futures side, reporting is triggered by ten or more occasions in a day of ten orders or cancels within one second, by fully automated order decisions, or by self-developed or custom software ([界面](https://m.jiemian.com/article/13185947.html); [同花顺](https://news.10jqka.com.cn/20250812/c670294168.shtml)). In practice, an individual running a Python strategy through a broker terminal or vn.py must file a report before trading; HFT fees and extra reporting will rarely apply at retail order rates, but aggressive limit-up bots that cancel and resubmit can still trip the abnormal-trading rules, and the public sources do not settle whether broker-hosted conditional or grid orders count as program trading. For tooling, 迅投's miniQMT exposes data and trading through the XtQuant Python package, which needs a running client ([迅投知识库](https://dict.thinktrader.net/nativeApi/start_now.html)); vn.py 4.5.0 offers CTP and securities gateways and a Qlib-style ML module ([vn.py](https://github.com/vnpy/vnpy)); and AKShare scrapes public sites and breaks when they change ([AKShare docs](https://akshare.akfamily.xyz/introduction.html)). Which brokers offer API terminals to individuals, and at what asset thresholds, could not be verified.

## Conclusion

What a 101/191/151 toolkit lacks is an evaluation layer, not more signals. Those three works generate hypotheses; OSAP, JKP, global-q, CH-4, the 469-anomaly China replication and the flex-mining null are the instruments that reveal which hypotheses repeat known themes, which are microcap artifacts and how much survives costs. For an individual in A-shares, factor knowledge is probably most valuable defensively: the micro-cap, turnover and price-volume-momentum exposures that drove the crashes of 2024 and 2026 are the exposures to measure and cap in any retail portfolio, including CB and ETF rotations whose holdings quietly load on them, whereas mining new formulas means entering a crowded institutional race without Level-2 data, execution speed or shorting.

The edges that remain for individuals are structural, and in China structural means regulatory. Shell value, IPO underpricing, cheap CB optionality, LOF premiums and the micro-cap liquidity premium were all created by rules and reshaped by rules in 2016, 2022, 2024, 2025 and 2026, so the regulatory calendar belongs in the risk model alongside prices. Globally, the robust retail approaches — trend, carry, option writing and volatility targeting — are risk premia that can lag for years; their binding constraint is behavioural rather than informational, and freedom from benchmarks is the individual's most important advantage. That points to a simple architecture: a slow, diversified global core in trend or managed futures and volatility-targeted allocation, China structural sleeves sized to their small capacity (credit-filtered CB rotation, subscriptions, reverse repo and commodity CTA), no capital in speed-based games, every rule fixed before trading, and every backtest Sharpe ratio halved before it is believed.
